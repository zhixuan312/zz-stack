# H11 — a day of measurement lived in a temp directory, and nothing said so

Every per-round score, every deviation classification, all 174 transcripts and the entire
variance study — 57MB — were written to a session-scoped scratch directory under `/private/tmp`.
What survived anywhere durable was the derived prose: five `*-quality.md` summaries and the
rubrics.

So the artifact could be read but not rebuilt, and no later question could be asked of the data.
"Compare ops-intent 1.0 and 1.1 on the work they both covered" was already impossible, and nothing
about the situation announced itself — the summaries looked complete, because a summary always
does.

Found by being asked whether the results were stored anywhere, not by anything in the apparatus.

## What it cost, nearly

The variance study is the clearest case: three repeats of eight requirements, each into a store
archived clean beforehand, producing the 0.436 noise floor that every threshold in this system is
derived from. Reproducing it costs 24 runs plus 24 judgements. One cleanup and that number becomes
a claim with nothing behind it.

## The fix, and the rule underneath it

`zz.eval`, `zz.eval_subject`, `zz.eval_score` and `zz.eval_finding` now hold it, written by
`eval-store`. All five evaluations were loaded: 104 subjects, one row per subject per dimension,
real and control side by side.

**One row per subject per dimension. Never an average.** An average over 30 pieces of work and an
average over 130 are not comparable, and a number collapsed at write time cannot be un-collapsed
at read time. Rows re-average over any subset, so comparing two versions stays a set intersection
rather than an impossibility — which is the whole reason the corpus can grow from 30 to 3,000
without invalidating what came before.

## The related rule, learned the same way

`zz.eval_score` points at `zz.eval_subject` — which CAPTURES team, initiative, path and content
hash at the moment of scoring — rather than joining to the team's document row.

`reindexTeam` deletes `zz.doc` rows whose files are gone. An earlier draft of the schema hung the
scores off `zz.doc(id)`, which would have meant a team archiving their own work silently destroyed
our measurement of our own skill. That is not hypothetical: resetting the evaluation store
archived 37 initiatives and reindex removed 132 rows in the same afternoon.

**A team deleting their work is a statement about their workspace. It is not permission to erase
our record of how our skill performed while doing it.**

It also fixes a quieter problem. Documents change after being scored — one initiative here holds
`spec.v1`, `spec.v2` and `spec.v4`. Joining a score to "the document" means it silently becomes a
score about text nobody ever judged. The content hash makes that impossible.
