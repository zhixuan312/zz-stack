# H7 — every quality score so far measured form, not quality (harness defect)

Four skills were measured across a thirty-requirement corpus and scored close to full marks.
The scores were real; what they measured was not what anybody wanted to know.

`eval-grade` asks three questions: does the document have the sections its role owes, did the
facts from the brief survive into it, and — for a selection — does the outcome contain the word
`not_possible` where the corpus says it should. Every one of those is answerable by a regular
expression, and every one of them is a question about SHAPE.

The failure this permits is not hypothetical, and it is the whole reason this file exists. A
selection that names all three building blocks for a requirement needing one carries every fact
in the brief, has every section the manifest asks for, and is wrong. It scores 100%. Nothing in
the grader can see the mistake, because the mistake is not in the document's form — the document
is beautifully formed — it is in the ANSWER, and no amount of pattern matching reads an answer.

Confirmed rather than suspected: the first thing the new judge was asked to derive was what
separates a strong selection from a weak one, and the five dimensions it produced from the
skill's own output — verdict accuracy, candour about limits, fidelity to the instance's real
configuration, specificity of rejections, grounding in prior findings — are all things only a
reader can score, and NONE of them were being scored by anything.

## What replaces it, and what does not

Both halves stay, and the split is the point.

The mechanical checks are the sanity floor. They are cheap, they never drift between runs, and
they catch the genuinely structural failures: a missing section, a dropped constraint, a document
that was never approved. Keeping them is not sentiment about sunk work — a judge asked to also
count headings spends its reading on counting.

The model is the quality ceiling: fit, accuracy, whether a plan could be built from, whether a
limit was stated flatly or softened. `eval-judge` does that half.

They are never added into one number. A single score lets a good rubric mark hide a missing
section, and lets a present section stand in for having said anything worth reading.

## The trap this creates, and the control that catches it

Handing the marking to a model does not by itself produce a measurement. A weak or lazy judge
does not return slightly worse scores — it returns confident uniform ones, which look exactly
like the too-good numbers that started this.

So every judged run has a scrambled twin: `--control` marks each document against a NEIGHBOUR's
requirement, changing nothing else. A judge that is reading collapses toward the floor. A judge
rewarding fluent prose scores about the same, and when it does, every quality number it produced
that day is worthless and gets thrown away rather than explained.

No quality score in this repository is to be quoted without its control beside it.
