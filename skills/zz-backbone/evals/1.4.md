# zz-backbone 1.4

Measured across a full corpus run — every ops-flow step ran inside it, so this is not a
probe but the whole body of work.

    every call the flow made, across every initiative
      1 refusal

## The refusal, and whose it is

    a block's write tool — only the HTTP client's own generic failure message

A bare status with no field, no value and no reason. Not a refusal a better
backbone could have avoided: nothing zz-backbone could have said would have made that call
different, because the answer does not say what was wrong with it.

**Refusals the flow could have avoided: 0 of 188.**

## What it actually spends its calls on

    155  core:skill_view
     23  core:initiative_status
      4  core:read_file
      6  block calls made while a step was between skills

Eighty-two per cent of the calls are loading the skill for the step about to run. That is what
this skill is for, and it is worth stating plainly because it also bounds what could go wrong: a
backbone that fails does so by loading the wrong skill or by not checking initiative state, and
neither happened in thirty-two initiatives.

## Sample size, honestly

Thirty-two initiatives is a real sample rather than an anecdote — the point at which a rate starts
being a property of the skill instead of a story about one run. A single refusal out of 188 is not
distinguishable from zero at this size, and both readings support the same conclusion: there is
nothing here to fix, and no evidence-backed change to propose.

## Why no change is proposed

An earlier round added three wait-and-close rules to this skill and they were reverted after a
controlled outage showed no improvement. On the noise floor now measured,
a change is called an improvement only when it clears the threshold for its sample. With zero
avoidable refusals there is no headroom for a change to clear anything — the correct outcome of
this round is no change, which is a result and not a failure to produce one.
