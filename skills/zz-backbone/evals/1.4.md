# zz-backbone 1.4

Measured across a full corpus run — every step of the flow ran inside it, so this is not a
probe but the whole body of work.

    every call the flow made, across every initiative
      1 refusal

## The refusal, and whose it is

    a block's write tool — only the HTTP client's own generic failure message

A bare status with no field, no value and no reason. Not a refusal a better
backbone could have avoided: nothing zz-backbone could have said would have made that call
different, because the answer does not say what was wrong with it.

**Refusals the flow could have avoided: none.**

## What it actually spends its calls on

    the large majority   core:skill_view
    most of the rest     core:initiative_status
    a handful            core:read_file
    a few                block calls made while a step was between skills

Four calls in five are loading the skill for the step about to run. That is what this skill is
for, and it is worth stating plainly because it also bounds what could go wrong: a backbone
that fails does so by loading the wrong skill or by not checking initiative state, and neither
happened anywhere in the corpus.

## Sample size, honestly

The corpus is a real sample rather than an anecdote — large enough that a rate starts being a
property of the skill instead of a story about one run. A single refusal at this size is not
distinguishable from zero, and both readings support the same conclusion: there is nothing
here to fix, and no evidence-backed change to propose.

## Why no change is proposed

An earlier round added three wait-and-close rules to this skill and they were reverted after a
controlled outage showed no improvement. On the noise floor now measured,
a change is called an improvement only when it clears the threshold for its sample. With zero
avoidable refusals there is no headroom for a change to clear anything — the correct outcome of
this round is no change, which is a result and not a failure to produce one.
